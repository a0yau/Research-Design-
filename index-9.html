02 / Why this matters

Research puzzle

The institutional change

New party-list rule

In 2021, parties had to include women and younger candidates jointly in at least 30% of their lists.

What we observe

Limited change in seats

Women’s presence in the Mazhilis changed little at the 2021 election, despite increased candidacy.

What we ask

Beyond numerical presence

Could the quota still affect women’s ability to participate in setting the legislative agenda on comparable terms with men?

Important: 30% was a combined candidate-list threshold—not 30% reserved seats for women.
Sources: IPU (2016, 2021); OSCE/ODIHR (2021) · No outcome claim is made03
03 / Theory from research

Literature review

What representation means

Seats are not the same as actions

Our focus is whether women can participate in legislative agenda-setting on equal terms with men—not simply occupy seats.

Pitkin (1967) · Paxton et al. (2025) · Childs & Krook (2009)

What quotas may change

Selection and policy attention

Quotas can alter recruitment or political expectations, but outcomes are not automatic.

Clayton (2021) · Franceschet & Piscopo (2008)

Why institutions matter

Access can enable or limit action

Party support, committees and informal rules affect MPs’ opportunities.

Chappell & Waylen (2013) · Heath et al. (2005)

Research gap: Test these mechanisms in the specific case of Kazakhstan’s combined quota and legislative agenda-setting.
Also: Paxton et al. (2025); Htun et al. (2013); Volden et al. (2013, 2018); Nurbayev et al. (2024); Aikenova (2026)04

Theory

05 / Falsifiable expectations

Hypotheses

H1

Recruitment pathway

If parties changed women’s nominations because of the quota, the change should be visible in lists and selection records.

Check → candidate lists, party records, dated decisions.
H2

Bills initiated by women

If recruitment or expectations changed, the first quota-era Mazhilis should show a higher bill-initiation rate per woman MP-year and a narrower gender gap relative to men.

Check → verified bill authors and time in office for women and men; evidence of a pathway.
H3

Institutional support

Any quota-linked increase should be stronger among women who had relevant party or committee resources before initiating bills.

Check → dated roles, documented support, interviews and bill timelines.
The hypotheses are predictions, not findings.06
06 / Key distinction

Representation

Formal

Having political rights

Can women legally vote, stand for election and hold office?

Descriptive

Being present

What number or share of MPs are women?

Substantive · outcome Y

Having an equal legislative voice

Do women have opportunities comparable to men to shape the legislative agenda?

Our focus: bills formally initiated by women MPs. Compare initiation rates with those of men; equal rates alone do not prove equal influence.
Pitkin (1967); Paxton et al. (2025, chapters 1 and 9)07
07 / Working definitions

Concepts

Institutional tool

A formal rule designed to shape political participation or decisions.

Electoral quota

A binding minimum-share rule for specified categories of candidates or seats.

Combined quota

In 2021, women and under-29 candidates together had to form at least 30% of a party list.

Substantive representation

Women’s legislative voices having comparable weight to men’s; bill-initiation rates are only a partial indicator of participation, not decision-making power.

Causal contribution

A documented role in producing an outcome, without being its only cause.

Institutional access

Prior, documented access to relevant party or committee support.

Tokenism

Symbolic inclusion without meaningful power; not inferred from numbers alone.

Women MPs

Women members of the Mazhilis identified in official records.

Mazhilis

Kazakhstan’s lower chamber of parliament.

Concepts follow the proposal; Sartori (1970) and Gerring (1999) guide conceptualisation.08

10 / Indicator justification

Why this indicator?

SeatsShow how many women are present, but not what they do.
Inquiries / speechesCapture oversight or visibility, not the formal introduction of draft laws.
Votes / laws passedDepend on party discipline and many decision-makers, not only the initiating MP.
Bill initiation ✓Records a specific MP’s formal attempt to introduce a legislative proposal. Comparing verified rates for women and men shows whether women participate in initiating bills at similar levels—even if some proposals are rejected.
It measures observable agenda-setting participation, not equal decision-making power or how seriously proposals are treated.
Franceschet & Piscopo (2008); Htun et al. (2013); Paxton et al. (2025, ch. 9)11
11 / Methods & case selection

Positivist design

Method 1 · Quantitative

Compare observable patterns

Women’s and men’s bill-initiation rates per MP-year, gender gaps, party and experience differences. Use count models only if records are complete enough.

Method 2 · Process tracing

Test the proposed mechanism

Trace the quota → party decisions → access → formally registered bill, using dated documents and structured interviews.

Case & units: Kazakhstan’s Mazhilis; MP-year, registered bill and party–election record. Full VI–VIII rosters, with party-list and other mandates distinguished.
Time structure: 2016–2021 baseline; 2021–2023 first-quota test; 2023–1 July 2026 separate extension: the electoral rules changed, and the Mazhilis ended on 1 July 2026.
PLS210 Week 10: observational comparison and theory-testing case study; IFES (2023); Constitution of Kazakhstan (2026, Art. 95)12

Evidence for each hypothesis

H1
Election law + party lists + selection recordsHow candidate nominations or list positions changed.
Evidence testCan the change be linked to the quota, rather than the party’s usual recruitment?
H2
Bill records + named initiators + women’s and men’s service datesWomen’s and men’s annualised bill-initiation rates, before/after.
Evidence testDid women’s rate and the gender gap change—with independent evidence for recruitment or signalling?
H3
Dated committee roles + party support + interviewsInstitutional resources available before a bill.
Evidence testDo resources precede and plausibly help the initiation, rather than follow earlier success?

14 / Practical checks

Data quality, access & feasibility

Access

What can be obtained?

Election laws and reports are public; the former Mazhilis website is archived. Individual initiators and party decisions may require requests.

Feasibility

Pilot before full collection

Audit sample bill files from each period. If author names are missing unevenly, do not calculate a misleading rate comparison.

Data quality

Check validity & reliability

Use pre-set initiator verification rules, independent double-checks, source logs, and separate flags for unknown authorship and verified zeros.

Quality rule: compare completeness across periods and genders; distinguish missing information from no activity. Report untestable hypotheses honestly.

16 / Rival explanations & limits

What could weaken the conclusion?

1 · Other events caused the change

COVID-19, the 2023 electoral reforms or shifts in party composition may explain activity after 2021.

2 · The quota did not change recruitment

Parties might have met the threshold through younger men or already-compliant lists.

3 · Records are incomplete

Unknown initiator names or inconsistent archives could distort comparisons across periods.

4 · Support is not the cause

Experienced MPs may receive influential committee posts because they were already active.

Responsible claim: evaluate the strength of evidence for quota-related pathways—not a clean experimental effect.
Threats: confounding · selection · reverse causality · missingness · 2023 legal change17
17 / Participant protection

Ethics

Voluntary participation

Explain the purpose and risks; get informed consent; allow refusal and withdrawal without penalty.

Confidentiality

Do not reveal sensitive party experiences without permission; use authorised or anonymised quotations.

Secure data storage

Encrypt recordings and transcripts; separate identity keys from analysis data; restrict access.

Research integrity & AI

Keep a source and coding log. Never paste identifiable participant information into AI tools.

Ethics approval and participant access are prerequisites—not assumed achievements.
